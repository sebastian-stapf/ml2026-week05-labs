6.1–6.3 · Functional and geometric margins

Confidence is distance to the line

Turn and shift the line \(w^\top x+b=0\). Each grey segment is a point's geometric margin \(\gamma^{(i)}\), its signed distance to the line. Then change the scale \(c\) of \((w,b)\): the line stays put, the functional margins \(\hat\gamma^{(i)}\) grow with \(c\), and the geometric margins do not change.

6.5 · Lagrange duality

The dual bound and complementary slackness

A one-dimensional problem small enough to see whole: minimise \(f(w)=\tfrac12w^2\) subject to \(w\ge t\), i.e. \(g(w)=t-w\le0\). For each multiplier \(\alpha\ge0\), the dual function \(\theta_D(\alpha)=\min_w\mathcal L(w,\alpha)\) is a lower bound on \(p^*\). Find the \(\alpha\) that makes the bound tight, then move \(t\) to switch the constraint between active and inactive.

\[\mathcal L(w,\alpha)=\tfrac12w^2+\alpha(t-w)\]\[\theta_D(\alpha)=\alpha t-\tfrac12\alpha^2\le p^*\]Section 6.5, KKT (6.3)–(6.7)

6.8 · Sequential minimal optimization

Two multipliers at a time

Discussion questions

Try each question on paper first. Then open the worked answer: it has a derivation, a live calculation you can change, and a one-line takeaway.

S1What happens to the margins when \((w,b)\) is replaced by \((cw,cb)\)?Consider \(x=(2,1)\), \(y=1\), \(w=(1,1)\), \(b=-1\) and any \(c>0\).Worked answer
  1. The classifier is unchanged: \(g(cw^\top x+cb)=g(w^\top x+b)\) because \(c>0\) does not change the sign.
  2. Functional margin: \(\hat\gamma=y(cw^\top x+cb)=c\,y(w^\top x+b)\). It scales linearly with \(c\).
  3. Geometric margin: \(\gamma=\hat\gamma/\|cw\|=c\,y(w^\top x+b)/(c\|w\|)\). The factor \(c\) cancels.
  4. Hence maximising \(\hat\gamma\) alone is meaningless, and the normalisation \(\hat\gamma=1\) in (6.8) is a free choice of scale.
Current result
TakeawayThe functional margin measures confidence only up to scale; the geometric margin is the scale-free distance.
S2Derive the geometric margin \(\gamma^{(i)}\) of a point.Use that \(w\) is orthogonal to the decision boundary.Worked answer
  1. \(w/\|w\|\) is the unit normal of the line. Starting at \(A=x^{(i)}\) and walking a distance \(\gamma^{(i)}\) against it reaches \(B=x^{(i)}-\gamma^{(i)}w/\|w\|\).
  2. \(B\) lies on the boundary: \(w^\top\big(x^{(i)}-\gamma^{(i)}\tfrac{w}{\|w\|}\big)+b=0\).
  3. Using \(w^\top w=\|w\|^2\): \(w^\top x^{(i)}+b-\gamma^{(i)}\|w\|=0\), so \(\gamma^{(i)}=\dfrac{w^\top x^{(i)}+b}{\|w\|}\).
  4. Multiplying by \(y^{(i)}\) makes it positive for correctly classified points of either class: \(\gamma^{(i)}=y^{(i)}\big((\tfrac{w}{\|w\|})^\top x^{(i)}+\tfrac{b}{\|w\|}\big)\).
Current result, w = (1, 2), b = −2, y = +1, A = (x₁, 1.5)
TakeawayThe geometric margin is the functional margin divided by \(\|w\|\): signed distance to the hyperplane.
S3Why does \(b^*=-\tfrac12\big(\max_{y^{(i)}=-1}w^{*\top}x^{(i)}+\min_{y^{(i)}=1}w^{*\top}x^{(i)}\big)\) hold?Use the data of Experiment 2. The answer updates when you drag points there.Worked answer
  1. With \(w^*\) fixed, the constraints read \(w^{*\top}x^{(i)}+b\ge1\) for positives and \(w^{*\top}x^{(i)}+b\le-1\) for negatives.
  2. The closest positive has projection \(m_+=\min_{y=1}w^{*\top}x\), the closest negative \(m_-=\max_{y=-1}w^{*\top}x\).
  3. At the optimum both margins are tight: \(m_++b^*=1\) and \(m_-+b^*=-1\).
  4. Adding the two equations gives \(m_++m_-+2b^*=0\), so \(b^*=-\tfrac12(m_-+m_+)\): the boundary lies halfway between the closest points of each class.
Current result from Experiment 2
TakeawayOnce \(w^*\) is known, \(b^*\) centres the boundary between the two classes' closest projections.
S4Derive the dual (6.12) and explain why most \(\alpha_i\) are zero.Start from the Lagrangian (6.9).Worked answer
  1. \(\mathcal L(w,b,\alpha)=\tfrac12\|w\|^2-\sum_i\alpha_i[y^{(i)}(w^\top x^{(i)}+b)-1]\) with \(\alpha_i\ge0\).
  2. \(\nabla_w\mathcal L=0\Rightarrow w=\sum_i\alpha_iy^{(i)}x^{(i)}\). \(\partial_b\mathcal L=0\Rightarrow\sum_i\alpha_iy^{(i)}=0\).
  3. Substituting gives \(W(\alpha)=\sum_i\alpha_i-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j}y^{(i)}y^{(j)}\alpha_i\alpha_j\langle x^{(i)},x^{(j)}\rangle\), maximised over \(\alpha\ge0\), \(\sum_i\alpha_iy^{(i)}=0\).
  4. Complementary slackness (6.5): \(\alpha_i\,[1-y^{(i)}(w^\top x^{(i)}+b)]=0\). Any point strictly outside the margin has a nonzero bracket, so \(\alpha_i=0\).
  5. Strong duality holds (convex objective, affine constraints, Slater), so \(W(\alpha^*)=\tfrac12\|w^*\|^2\).
Current result from Experiment 2
TakeawayThe data enter the dual only through inner products, and only the support vectors carry weight. These two facts make kernels practical.
S5Where does the box constraint \(0\le\alpha_i\le C\) come from?Form the soft-margin Lagrangian with multipliers \(\alpha_i\) and \(r_i\) for \(\xi_i\ge0\).Worked answer
  1. \(\mathcal L=\tfrac12w^\top w+C\sum_i\xi_i-\sum_i\alpha_i[y^{(i)}(x^{(i)\top}w+b)-1+\xi_i]-\sum_ir_i\xi_i\), with \(\alpha_i,r_i\ge0\).
  2. \(\partial\mathcal L/\partial\xi_i=C-\alpha_i-r_i=0\Rightarrow\alpha_i=C-r_i\le C\).
  3. The \(w\) and \(b\) conditions are unchanged, so the dual objective is the same \(W(\alpha)\). The \(\xi_i\) terms cancel, and only the bound \(\alpha_i\le C\) is new.
  4. Complementarity gives (6.16)–(6.18): \(\alpha_i=0\Rightarrow y^{(i)}f(x^{(i)})\ge1\); \(0<\alpha_i0\)).
Current result from Experiment 4
Takeaway\(C\) caps how much any single point can pull on \(w\). An outlier can contribute at most \(C\,y^{(i)}x^{(i)}\).
S6Why must SMO change two \(\alpha\)'s, and what are \(L\) and \(H\)?Hold \(\alpha_3,\dots,\alpha_n\) fixed and look at the pair \((\alpha_1,\alpha_2)\).Worked answer
  1. From \(\sum_i\alpha_iy^{(i)}=0\): \(\alpha_1=-y^{(1)}\sum_{i\ge2}\alpha_iy^{(i)}\). With \(\alpha_2,\dots,\alpha_n\) fixed, \(\alpha_1\) cannot move.
  2. For a pair, \(\alpha_1y^{(1)}+\alpha_2y^{(2)}=\zeta\): the feasible set is a line segment inside the box \([0,C]^2\).
  3. If \(y^{(1)}\ne y^{(2)}\): \(\alpha_2-\alpha_1\) is fixed, so \(L=\max(0,\alpha_2-\alpha_1)\), \(H=\min(C,C+\alpha_2-\alpha_1)\).
  4. If \(y^{(1)}=y^{(2)}\): \(\alpha_1+\alpha_2\) is fixed, so \(L=\max(0,\alpha_1+\alpha_2-C)\), \(H=\min(C,\alpha_1+\alpha_2)\).
Current result, C = 1
TakeawayTwo variables are the smallest set that can move while keeping \(\sum_i\alpha_iy^{(i)}=0\), and the update along that line has a closed form.
